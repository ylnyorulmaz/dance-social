About Dance Social

Dance happens in rooms. The internet should help us get there.

Dance Social is an invitation-led social network for dancers, communities and events. It is being built around a simple idea: technology should make real dance communities easier to find, join, remember and sustain — not turn them into another endless content feed.

A digital clubhouse for people who dance: identity, community, conversation and events in one place.

Why it exists

Dance communities are social. Their tools are fragmented.

Today a dancer may discover an event on Instagram, discuss it in WhatsApp, follow an instructor somewhere else, search Facebook for an old announcement, and still have no clear view of the actual local dance scene.

Generic platforms are good at broadcasting content. They are much worse at preserving the social structure of a dance community: who dances what, which communities exist, what is happening tonight, which events repeat every week, and where useful discussions live after the chat has moved on.

People

A persistent dancer identity built around dance styles, city, communities and real participation.

Communities

Places for local scenes, styles, schools, interests and persistent conversations that do not vanish in chat.

Events

A structured event graph for socials, practicas, milongas, workshops, classes and festivals.

Who it is for

Not just experts. Not just one scene.

Dance Social is intended for active dancers, complete beginners, instructors, DJs, organizers, schools, venues and eventually festivals and dance-related businesses.

The product is cross-dance by design: Lindy Hop, Salsa, Bachata, Tango, Balboa, West Coast Swing, Solo Jazz, Kizomba, Hip-Hop, K-pop, folk, contemporary and other community-driven dance forms can coexist without being forced into one culture.

The first launch can still be deliberately small. A dense real-world community is more valuable than a large but disconnected signup count.

What it does

One network, several useful layers.

Dancer profiles

Dance styles, city, bio, posts, follows, connections, communities and events.

Community spaces

Feeds, questions, members, recurring activity and public/private membership.

Event discovery

What is happening tonight, this week, in this city, for this dance.

Social graph

Follow dancers, discover mutual connections and carry your dance network across communities.

Organizer tools

Later: recurring events, attendee management, analytics, reminders and ticketing.

Useful AI

Clearly labeled bots for event discovery, practice, music and moderation — never fake humans.

Product principles

Designed to strengthen the room, not replace it.

People over content volume.
Communities over creators.
Real-world participation over screen time.
Persistent knowledge over disappearing chat.
Trust over maximum reach.
Understandable feeds over opaque ranking.

What this is not

It is not intended to be a TikTok clone, a dating app wearing dance shoes, or another product optimized to keep people scrolling forever. The useful outcome is often that you close the app and go dance.

Why invitation-led?

Trust matters in small real-world scenes.

Dance communities are unusually physical and social. People meet face to face. Event attendance can reveal where someone will be at a particular time. That makes trust and privacy more important than raw signup velocity.

The early invitation model is meant to reduce spam and random abuse while preserving a clear path for genuine beginners. Being new to dance should never disqualify someone from joining the community.

Still early. Already moving.

The current version is an interactive prototype. If the idea resonates, try it, send feedback, or help keep the experiment moving.
